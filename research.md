My research interests focus on applying genetic and molecular approaches to understand how animals adapt and respond to climate change. 

This interest started when I was introduced to phenotypic plasticity while working with aphids in the Brisson Lab at the University of Rochester. Now, I focus on plasticity that occurs within an oyster's lifetime (within-generation carryover effects) in the Gignoux-Wolfsohn Lab at the Unviersity of Massachusetts Lowell.

## PhD Dissertation
### Molecular mechanisms of climate change stressor-induced carryover effects in eastern oysters
My research is part of a collaborative project with other labs aiming to understand carryover effects in eastern oysters (*Crassostrea virginica*). Through mesocosm experiments, we exposed juvenile oysters to two rounds of treatments with manipulated water (dissolved oxygen and warming) in a fully factorial design. 

![CE experimental design](/images/ce_experimentalDesign.png)

I specifically focus on the molecular mechanisms behind carryover effects through epigenetics (specifically DNA methylation) and transcriptomics.

#### Chapter 1: Investigate the effects of multiple exposures to a single stressor on methylation in oyster whole-body tissues
The first chapter of my dissertation thesis used MethylRAD-seq to understand the methylome of whole body tissues in juvenile oysters exposed to either hypoxia (<2 mg/L) or normoxia. I found that early life environments have a lasting impact on the epigenome and that the timing of stress elicits unique response strategies, which highlights potential targets of resilience for oysters! This resulted in the [publication of my first first-authored paper in BMC genomics](https://doi-org.umasslowell.idm.oclc.org/10.1186/s12864-026-12808-6)! The analysis can be found in [this repository](https://github.com/jgmcdonough/CE18_methylRAD_analysis).
